# 02 — Post Structure Templates

Every Frachtwerk post follows one of six structural patterns. Choose the pattern before writing a single word.

**First decision: is this content marketing or opinion?** Patterns A–E are useful when the post's job is to inform (a report, an event, a capability). Pattern F is different in kind, not just in topic — it exists to argue a position from `06_point_of_view.md`. When in doubt, default to F: it produces posts that sound less like brand content and more like someone with a stake in the industry saying what they think.

---

## Pattern A — The Data Hook
*Use for: industry statistics, reports, market data*

```
[STRIKING NUMBER or STAT] — [one-line context that reframes it]

[2–3 sentences: what the data reveals that people aren't seeing]

[Industry-specific implication for the Disponent / Spedition]

[3–5 bullets with emoji or → arrows: concrete observations or signals]

[Bridge to Frachtwerk — 1 sentence, must feel like a logical consequence]

[CTA — link or call to action]

[Hashtags — 5–7]
```

**Annotated example:**
> *„17,8 % der arbeitsfähigen Weltbevölkerung nutzt KI. In deutschen Dispoabteilungen? Vermutlich deutlich weniger."*
> ← Hook: stat + rhetorical contrast immediately anchors the tension

> *„Microsoft hat am 7. Mai den Global AI Diffusion Report 2026 Q1 veröffentlicht."*
> ← Source named immediately. Credibility established.

> *„🌍 +1,5 Prozentpunkte... 🇦🇪 VAE führen mit 70,1 %..."*
> ← Bullets skim-readable for LinkedIn. Emoji flags/icons work as visual anchors.

> *„Genau hier setzen wir mit FRACHTWERK an."*
> ← Product tie-in arrives AFTER the data has built the case.

## Pattern B — The News Event Pivot
*Use for: regulatory news, political events, tech shutdowns, industry news*

```
[Event description — specific date, what happened, factual]

[Most people see it as X. For a Spedition, it is Y.]

[2–3 sentences: the real operational implication]

[The Frachtwerk angle — why this proves our design principle (e.g. Datenhoheit)]

[Closing aphorism or reframe — one sentence, punchy]

[Optional CTA to blog]

[Hashtags — 5–7]
```

**Annotated example:**
> *„Letzten Freitag wurde eines der leistungsfähigsten KI-Modelle der USA über Nacht weltweit abgeschaltet."*
> ← Opens with an event. No opinion yet. Just fact.

> *„Für die meisten ist das eine Tech-Schlagzeile. Für eine Spedition ist es etwas anderes..."*
> ← Pivot: now the post speaks only to this audience. Everyone else can leave.

> *„Souveränität ist kein Fahnenschwenken. Es ist Risikomanagement."*
> ← Closing aphorism. Short. Quotable. Could be pulled as a standalone graphic.

---

## Pattern C — The Early Warning
*Use for: supply chain risk, carrier insolvency wave, structural market deterioration*

```
[Alarming headline stat — bold, verifiable]

[Most people read this as a supplier problem. Reframe: it reaches buyers too.]

[Time delay framing: "with 6–12 months delay..."]

[Numbered or arrow-bulleted warning signals — actionable, specific]

[What the smart response looks like — not our product yet, just the category of action]

[Bridge to Frachtwerk as one of the tools for that response]

[CTA to deeper content]

[Hashtags]
```

---

## Pattern D — Capability Showcase
*Use for: showcasing what Frachtwerk or a partner company can do; may be bilingual*

```
[Dramatic opening image/scenario — sensory, specific, not generic]

[Why conventional approaches fail here]

[What we do instead — three qualities, emoji-formatted]
🛠️ [Quality 1]: [brief proof]
🛡️ [Quality 2]: [brief proof]
🌱 [Quality 3]: [brief proof]

[Hashtags — technical + industry]
```

**Rule for this pattern:** No product names, no prices, no CTA to demo. Let the image and capabilities speak. Product-forward posts underperform on LinkedIn unless the capability is already proven.

---

## Pattern E — Insolvency / Obituary
*Use for: named company failures, structural exits from the market*

```
[Bold number] [stark one-line consequence]

[Named companies — real names, real numbers: employees, years operating]

[Why they fell: systemic, not individual failure]

[What this means for the market going forward]

[Frachtwerk positioning: who we're building for and why now]

[No CTA needed — post stands alone]

[Hashtags — English if the insight is globally relevant]
```

**Rule:** Never editorialize about the companies that failed. Respect and gravity. The numbers speak.

---

## Pattern F — Opinion / Market Critique
*Use for: taking a public position on how the market operates, a trend, a widely-repeated claim, or conventional wisdom Frachtwerk disagrees with. This is the default pattern for posts whose main job is opinion, not information.*

```
[The contested question or claim — stated plainly, as something a chunk of the market believes or does]

[Why that's wrong, incomplete, or misleading — 2–4 sentences, prose, no bullets]

[The belief from 06_point_of_view.md being defended, stated as a flat claim]

[Evidence or first-hand grounding — a number, a named event, an operational detail that makes the claim earned rather than asserted]

[Optional: what the smart alternative looks like — category-level, not necessarily Frachtwerk]

[Optional, only if earned: Frachtwerk as the practical consequence of the belief — may be entirely absent]

[No CTA required. If one exists, it invites discussion, not a demo.]

[Hashtags — 3–5, brand hashtag optional]
```

**Annotated example (built from a real published post):**
> *„17,8 % der arbeitsfähigen Weltbevölkerung nutzt KI. In deutschen Dispoabteilungen? Vermutlich deutlich weniger."*
> ← Opens on the contested question: is the industry actually adopting AI, or just talking about it.

> *„Asien zieht massiv an, sobald die Sprachmodelle in der Landessprache funktionieren... die technische Barriere ist gefallen. Was fehlt, ist die operative Integration."*
> ← This is belief #8 from the point-of-view bank, applied to this specific report.

> *„Wer jetzt nicht in produktive KI-Anwendungen geht, fällt nicht morgen zurück — sondern hat es bereits."*
> ← Closing aphorism carries the opinion. No CTA needed to make the point land.

**Rule for this pattern:** Default to prose, not bullets. Opinion reads as argument, not as a scannable feature list — save `→` bullets and emoji-tagged lists for Patterns A–C where they're doing real work (data points, warning signals). A Pattern F post with five bulleted takeaways has drifted back into content marketing.

---

## Universal Structural Rules

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Hook (line 1)** | Must work as a standalone sentence when pulled out of context. Test: would someone screenshot just this line? |
| **Paragraph length** | Maximum 3 sentences per paragraph on LinkedIn. Prefer 1–2. |
| **Bullets** | Use `→` for actionable items. Use emoji (🛠️🛡️🌱📈) for benefit/feature lists. Never mix the two in one post. Default to prose (no bullets) in Pattern F. |
| **Frachtwerk mention** | Optional. In Patterns A–E, appears at most once, never twice, never in the first third. In Pattern F, may be absent entirely — the belief being defended must be what justifies the mention, not the post's obligation to reference the product. |
| **Closing aphorism** | Optional but powerful. One sentence. Subject-verb-object. No subordinate clauses. In Pattern F, this is often the whole payoff — the post can end here with no product mention and no CTA. |
| **CTA** | Optional, maximum one per post. In Patterns A–C, usually a link to deeper content. In Pattern F, prefer none, or a low-key invitation to disagree in the comments rather than a link. |
| **Hashtags** | 5–7 in Patterns A–E. 3–5 in Pattern F, brand hashtag (`#frachtwerk`) optional. On their own line(s) at the end. Never inline mid-sentence. |
